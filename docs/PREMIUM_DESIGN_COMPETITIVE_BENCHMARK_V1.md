# Choice Kingdom — Premium Competitive Visual Benchmark v1

Date: 2026-09-17

## Purpose

Premium design decisions are informed by current visual references from high-quality narrative/premium mobile games. References are used as design evidence, not as assets or templates to copy.

## Reference findings

### Monument Valley / Monument Valley 2

The official Google Play presentation and studio material emphasize an art-led experience in which environments and interactions carry the visual identity. The design is deliberately restrained: minimal UI, strong composition, distinctive geometry, and screens that can stand as individual pieces of art. citeturn1search5turn0search6turn0search4

**Choice Kingdom takeaway:** every decision scene should have a clear composition before UI is added; decoration must serve the scene rather than become a second competing layer.

### Florence

Florence uses bespoke illustrated vignettes and touch interactions to make the physical act of interacting part of the storytelling. Its Google Play description positions it as a premium, one-time-purchase narrative experience. citeturn0search13turn0news27

**Choice Kingdom takeaway:** interaction feedback should communicate narrative state, not merely button mechanics. A selected decision should feel like a deliberate human action.

### Reigns

Reigns demonstrates the strength of a single, highly legible decision grammar: cards, immediate binary action, persistent kingdom pressure, and consequences that emerge over time. The current Google Play listing still positions the game as an offline premium interactive story and describes faction balance as a core visual/gameplay relationship. citeturn2search3turn2search4

**Choice Kingdom takeaway:** the choice itself must remain unmistakable and fast to understand. However, Choice Kingdom will use authored narrative panels and cinematic composition rather than copying the Reigns card/swipe presentation.

### Citizen Sleeper

Citizen Sleeper's art direction was intentionally simple but evocative, using stylization and lived-in character/world details to make the player fill in some of the world mentally rather than over-describing every surface. citeturn0search12

**Choice Kingdom takeaway:** premium does not mean maximum visual detail. Detail should be concentrated where it creates identity, emotion and place.

## Design principles adopted

1. **Art before chrome.** Establish focal composition first; UI frames the story.
2. **One dominant action.** The choice must remain the strongest interactive object on an Event scene.
3. **Editorial rhythm.** Headline, narrative, metadata and choice controls should read like a composed page rather than a generic dashboard.
4. **Material restraint.** Use paper, stone, wood, cloth, seal and aged-metal cues sparingly; avoid glossy fantasy UI.
5. **Human specificity.** Characters need recognizable silhouettes, expressions and contextual details.
6. **Consequences as state change.** Avoid reward explosions; use controlled transitions that feel like the chronicle has recorded the decision.
7. **Touch as storytelling.** Press/focus/resolve states should communicate commitment and transition.
8. **Mobile-first negative space.** Small screens need breathing room around the focal narrative and decision controls.
9. **Accessibility is part of visual polish.** Focus, target size, state semantics and RTL are designed rather than bolted on.
10. **Original identity.** No direct copying of competitor artwork, logos, layouts, characters or branded motifs.

## P6 application

The current P6 surface therefore evolves toward an **editorial decision chamber**: cinematic scene header + restrained metadata + high-contrast narrative title + two or more authored decision controls + a quiet state line + controlled transition into consequence.

The benchmark does **not** increase P6's percentage by itself. Implementation and rendered verification remain the gates.
