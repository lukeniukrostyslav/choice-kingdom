# Choice Kingdom — Gameplay Pacing & Replay Design

## Goal

The campaign must feel like a chain of decisions rather than a spreadsheet with story attached. A player should regularly ask: "Did I choose the right thing?" and later: "Wait — was that because of something I did twenty turns ago?"

## Turn rhythm

A normal sequence uses a four-beat rhythm:

1. **Pressure** — a concrete problem demands attention.
2. **Choice** — two or more defensible responses.
3. **Consequence** — immediate feedback without explaining every hidden variable.
4. **Echo** — later event reveals what the choice actually changed.

Do not use more than three consecutive low-stakes resource-management events without an interpersonal or narrative complication.

## Campaign pacing

### Opening: turns 1–6
Purpose: teach the player that choices affect people and institutions, not only numbers.

- E01 establishes access to power.
- E02 introduces institutional uncertainty.
- E03 introduces the first resource trade-off.
- E04 introduces the old regime's hidden cost.
- E05/E06 introduce competing power centers.

The player should leave the opening with at least one ally and one unresolved concern.

### Expansion: turns 7–14
Purpose: make the kingdom feel responsive.

Merchant, military, noble, welfare, and street-network events should interleave. The player should start seeing callbacks to earlier decisions.

At least one event in this range must produce a benefit that later becomes a liability.

### Mystery: turns 15–24
Purpose: replace simple optimization with uncertainty.

The hidden ledger chain should make the player reconsider earlier assumptions. The game should avoid revealing a single villain too early.

At least three pieces of evidence should be available through different relationship routes so that replay changes how the mystery is solved.

### Crisis: turns 25–34
Purpose: force prioritization.

Multiple systems should fail simultaneously. The player cannot maximize everything. Earlier investments should create distinct survival strategies.

The crisis should contain at least one event where the player's best historical relationship becomes more important than the highest resource value.

### Ending: turns 35+
Purpose: convert the run's accumulated behavior into a believable political outcome.

The ending must be earned by patterns. A single final button must never override the preceding campaign.

## Branch-density rules

Every major choice should satisfy at least two of these:

- changes a resource;
- changes a relationship;
- creates/removes a flag;
- changes the next available event;
- schedules a delayed consequence;
- changes information available later;
- changes an ending eligibility condition.

At least one choice in every 5-event window should create a delayed effect.

At least one event in every 7-event window should have a relationship consequence.

At least one event in every 10-event window should reveal new information rather than only modify resources.

## Anti-repetition rules

Reject content when:

- both choices differ only by resource signs;
- the same character asks the same type of favor three times in a row;
- a consequence is fully explained immediately and therefore cannot surprise the player;
- the player can identify the optimal choice solely by comparing resource deltas;
- an event does not change future content, state, information, or relationship;
- a branch exists only cosmetically and converges immediately with no remembered effect.

## Hidden-information rules

The game may hide:

- the true reason an NPC asks for something;
- whether a delayed consequence will occur;
- which character benefits from a policy;
- which ending condition a choice contributes toward.

The game must never hide basic control feedback. Players should understand what their immediate choice visibly did.

## Relationship design

Relationships are not friendship meters. Each character represents a political philosophy and a practical capability.

A high relationship should unlock capabilities, not merely compliments. A low relationship should create different information, obstacles, or political alternatives rather than simply reducing a number.

## Replay design

A second run should produce at least three meaningful differences from the first run when the player deliberately chooses a different philosophy:

1. a different event or event order;
2. a different character route or information source;
3. a different ending candidate.

The first replay should teach the player that the game is systemic. Subsequent replays should reveal increasingly hidden interactions.

## Balance philosophy

Resources are not score bars. They are pressure indicators.

A resource near 100 is not always better: excessive power, for example, can trigger fear; excessive security can increase military influence; excessive commercial growth can create monopoly risk.

The engine should therefore support soft bands and threshold events rather than treating every resource as a simple win condition.

## Production content target

The first commercial campaign should contain 40–60 authored events with enough alternate routes that a normal run encounters only a subset. The engine must support substantially more without redesign.
